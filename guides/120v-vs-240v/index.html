Electrical guide

120V vs 240V saunas.

The voltage label is only the beginning. Circuit amperage, receptacle type, heater load and manufacturer instructions determine what installation actually requires.

Why 120V models are attractive

Some home infrared saunas—and a small number of specialized hybrid designs—operate on 120V power. That can make installation simpler when the manufacturer permits connection to an appropriate existing receptacle. “120V,” however, should not automatically be interpreted as “any outlet.” The required amperage and whether the circuit must be dedicated still matter.

Why traditional saunas are often 240V

Electric rock heaters commonly use higher power than plug-in infrared cabins. Many residential traditional sauna heaters therefore use 240V circuits and require hardwiring or a specific electrical arrangement. Heater size, room volume and local electrical code all affect the final installation.

Use the database filter: Infinite Sauna stores voltage, amperage and plug information as separate fields when those details are verified. This is more useful than filtering on voltage alone.

Do not size electrical work from a comparison website

The comparison database is a research tool, not an electrical plan. Before installing a sauna, use the current installation manual for the exact model and heater configuration. Where required, have a qualified electrician confirm circuit sizing, conductor sizing, overcurrent protection and receptacle or hardwire requirements.
